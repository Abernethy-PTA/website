## What this is about

“Rightsizing” is coming to Portland Public Schools. This is the term the district is using for potential school closures and consolidation. This moment is the result of trends over years: declining enrollment plus school funding that is not keeping pace with school needs.

## Our goal

The Abernethy PTA aligns with other PPS communities in demanding a methodical, transparent, accountable approach to district rightsizing. We want what's best for Abernethy students and all PPS students. Abernethy is a strong neighborhood school and we want to see it stay strong into the future.

## 1. Learn what’s happening

PPS has released its potential scenarios to close schools and relocate programs.

- **See [what PPS is sharing](https://www.pps.net/about/portland-public-schools-information/rightsize/rightsize)** about its plans for rightsizing.
- **Review [the PPS family guide for Abernethy](/rightsizing/pps-family-guide-abernethy.pdf)** (PDF) showing the proposed changes and their direct impact on our school.
- **Watch the [PPS Board's meeting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zOUH2Jq7Owo)** when they heard details of the district's proposals and started discussion.
- **Educate yourself.** This [community-made PPS Dashboard](https://ppsdata.info/) shows how closure might affect different schools across our city. [This PPS map](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1I4Vh8XCyjGLafcE2KQ4pRaIB6iyoOkv9/view?usp=drive_link) shows building utilization across the district. Utilization is how efficiently and effectively schools use their buildings relative to their student capacity (ex: are buildings overcrowded, underused, or right-sized?). It’s one of the factors district leaders will consider.
- **Follow updates** in Principal Dunn’s weekly newsletter and on [Konstella](https://www.konstella.com/p/abernethy), the PTA’s app for connecting directly with families.

## The action we want to see from our PPS leaders and lawmakers

- **A clear pathway for accountability at the state and local level**
    - Recent events (e.g., damning audit results about the high school renovation contracts) have inspired a lack of faith in our public school leadership.
    - Changing messages from district leaders cause us to question the transparency of this process.
- **A strategic plan around the impact of PPS school closures** on:
    - Students: class sizes, resources, academic development, etc.
    - Schools: funding remodels and renovations, selling assets, etc.
    - Communities: how PPS will work with community partners to fill the gaps that school buildings leave behind

In the end, it is our students who will bear the cost of the choices adults make: whether rightsizing or maintaining the status quo. District leaders must demonstrate that they are accountable by sharing the burden our students are facing. **We all want to get rightsizing right.**

## What we want for our school - Abernethy community discussion

*At our October community meeting, PTA families discussed rightsizing, and this summary shares what we heard:*

As a PTA, we believe that our educational system needs to change. We support parents advocating for what they feel is best for their students. The current path our schools are on is unsustainable and we know we cannot continue the “status quo.” 

We want well-resourced schools for our kids and for district leaders to listen to us about what they look like. This includes:
- caps on classroom sizes
- unblended grades
- full-time on-site specials teachers
- a full-time on-site counselor
- safe, accessible, and healthy building structures
- challenge/TAG groups and reading supports 

We want transparency and mutuality in this process. We want PPS leaders to listen to impacted communities and provide support before, during, and after transitions, especially for students moving between schools. We want clear communication about budgets and resource allocation. 

We want to know that district leaders understand the full consequences of their proposals, the actions required to achieve the intended benefits, and will stand with us through these transitions.

We encourage Abernethy families to [raise their voices](https://supportabernethy.org/programs/advocacy/) and express their own views on the rightsizing process by contacting district leaders. We are one community with many voices.  

We are here to support you in raising your voices for our community. We are already actively engaging with our fellow SE PTAs in discussions with the School Board, our elected representatives, the District and our school leadership. We want to understand your perspective and empower action.

For more information about how rightsizing may affect our school, review  [the PPS family guide for Abernethy](/rightsizing/pps-family-guide-abernethy.pdf), which shows the proposed changes and their direct impact on our school as published by the district.
